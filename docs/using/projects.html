Working with Babbage

Projects

A project bundles conversations around one subject. What you set at project level applies to every conversation in it: your instructions, your sources and any linked folder. You make one with the + next to Projects in the sidebar, or with New project on the projects page (View all under Projects); a name is all you need. In the sidebar a project unfolds on its most recent conversations when you click its folder icon, and Pin in its menu moves it to Pinned at the top.

On the project page you start conversations with the input bar at the top. On the right is the column About this project, with everything the project shares.

Instructions

Under Instructions you put what should apply in every conversation in this project: a role, a tone, fixed guidelines. 8,000 characters at most.

When they contradict, the order decides. The dialog says so itself: "They apply to every conversation in this project; the agent's identity comes first, these instructions come after it." The agent's identity comes first, then the project instructions: the more specific layer comes later and therefore wins.

The sources of a project

Under Sources is what every conversation in this project can see: documents and linked folders. There is no separate working-folder section; a folder is simply a source.

In a conversation they appear in the group The whole project. You can look at them there and switch them off per conversation, but renaming and deleting you do on the project page: that is where they belong. The other way round, you lift a source out of a conversation with Move to the project. How that list works is on Sources.

Conversations in and out of a project

A conversation you start on the project page belongs to it right away. An existing conversation you drag onto the project in the sidebar, or you pick Move to project in the menu of that conversation. Getting it out again you do with Remove from project.

There is no confirmation, so know what it does: unlinking or moving makes all project context lapse. The instructions no longer apply, the project sources and the project folder are gone, and the exceptions you had made on project sources in that conversation disappear with them. The conversation and its messages stay in full.

Deleting a whole project does ask for confirmation, and it tells you case by case what happens to the conversations, the working folder and the documents.

Answer only from the sources

Above the sources list is a switch with that name. Turn it on and every conversation in this project behaves differently. The line underneath says what it promises:

The agent uses only what is in this project and says so when the answer is not in there. No web searches, no MCP servers, no file changes or shell, no research runs and no scheduled tasks.

On top of that, the agent can no longer search your other conversations, and of what it remembers about you only your profile stays in the prompt; the remembered facts are answer material and stay out.

Since there are no modes to fall back from, the contract simply takes those capabilities away: nothing on your disk can change, and the deep research action is greyed out with the reason "A research run does not work with “Answer only from the sources” on." Reading a linked folder is still allowed, and it is one of the ways an answer gets its source reference.

While it is on, a pill sits in the bar under the input bar: Sources only. Click it and it lists exactly what lapses, so the promise is readable where you work instead of only on the project page.

What the setting does not promise is stated literally as soon as you turn it on:

What the model knows from its training is not switched off here; no more than the agent's identity, your project instructions, what has been remembered about you, your active skills and today's date. Those are simply in the prompt. What the contract does do is close off every route along which the agent fetches something extra while answering.

With a linked folder the promise is smaller, and the sources panel says so: "With a linked folder the promise covers what has been read, not everything in it."

Where the answer comes from

The agent refers to its source for every claim, and those references are clickable: a document opens on the passage itself, an attachment jumps to the message you sent it with, a folder opens the sources panel. If the answer is not in your sources, it says so, with two ways out: add a source, or turn the setting off.

Under the message is what came of that contract. There are three labels, and an answer that comes entirely from your sources deliberately carries none:

  • partly from your sources. Part of it comes from your sources; for the rest the agent said it is not in there.
  • not in your sources. Your sources do not cover the answer. This is the only label where the app names an action itself: add a source, or turn the setting off on the project page.
  • not grounded, in warning colour. The answer does not refer to a source, or refers to something that is not in there. The app says one thing about it: read it with extra care.

That last label also lands on an answer that makes no claim at all, such as a counter-question. Then nothing is wrong; there is simply nothing to refer to.

Turning the setting off again

The same switch, and nothing is left behind: the web tools and MCP servers work again, writing and research runs are possible again, and scheduled tasks can run again. Answers you already got keep their label, because that is about the turn they were given in.
